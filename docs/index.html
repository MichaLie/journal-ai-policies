Read this first

You have almost certainly not done anything wrong.

Of the 100 highest-ranked journals surveyed in 2023, 87% had published guidance on generative AI and just one prohibited using it to help write a manuscript outright. What nearly all of them prohibited was something different: listing AI as an author. Describe permitted use plainly. A disclosure does not make a prohibited use acceptable.

Ganjavi C, et al. Publishers' and journals' instructions to authors on use of generative artificial intelligence in academic and scientific publishing: bibliometric analysis. BMJ 2024;384:e077192. doi:10.1136/bmj-2023-077192 · PMID 38296328

Where the real lines are

The lines are concrete. Do not invent citations. Do not present generated content as your data. Do not upload an unpublished manuscript while reviewing it unless the journal explicitly authorizes the tool and use. For language help, follow the journal’s disclosure rule and carry on.

If you write in English as a second language, one more thing

Published policy and screening practice can differ. Across 17 cardiovascular journals studied, 15 used AI-detection software and one said so publicly. You should know that these detectors are unreliable, and unreliable in a specific direction: a widely cited study found they wrongly flagged 61% of essays written by non-native English speakers, against almost none by native speakers. That is a defect in the detectors, not in your writing. It is also why a clear, dated note of what you actually did is worth more to you than to anyone else.

Laffaye TA, et al. Artificial intelligence for manuscript writing: policies and implementation in cardiovascular journals. Cardiovasc Diagn Ther 2025. doi:10.21037/cdt-2025-381 · PMID 41216259

Liang W, et al. GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers. Patterns 2023;4(7):100779. doi:10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779 · PMID 37521038

A policy page, an announcement and a later revision may carry different dates. Springer Nature announced its risk framework on 10 September 2026, although it was already present in the 4 August archive held here. Use the current policy for your decision, and keep a dated copy of the wording you followed.

"Fixing my English" and "rewriting my text" are not the same thing

Start with what changed. Correcting grammar, tightening phrasing and fixing article use is language editing. Adding arguments, interpretations or conclusions is substantive writing. Translating a draft is a separate activity. Policies draw these boundaries differently; describe the work plainly and use your journal’s rule.

If the distinction is unclear, describe what the tool actually did and check the journal’s rule. A declaration helps readers understand the work, but does not override a prohibition.

Ans M, et al. The Presence and Nature of AI-Use Disclosure Statements in Medical Education Journals. Perspect Med Educ 2026;15:212-225. doi:10.5334/pme.2431 · PMID 41800434

I used AI to draft or substantially rewrite sectionsdeclare it

Check that your journal permits the kind of drafting you used. Then name the tool, version and sections, and explain your own verification. A declaration describes the work; it does not make a prohibited use acceptable.

example wording
[Tool and version] was used to draft [which sections], accessed [date]. All generated text was verified against the primary sources and substantially revised by the authors, who take full responsibility for the content.

Name the sections. A statement saying only "AI was used in preparing this manuscript" is the kind editors describe as uninformative — one study found disclosures averaging 43 words and describing the use only superficially.

I used AI to generate or retouch a figure showing my actual datacheck before you do it

A plot made from your data is different from an altered experimental image. Keep the underlying data and reproducible plotting method. Check the journal’s image rules before using AI to retouch a blot, micrograph or scan.

example wording
[Tool and version] was used to produce the plots in Figure [n] from the underlying data, accessed [date]. No image content was added, removed or repositioned; source data are available at [location].

Only use this if it is true. Generating a chart from real data is routine; altering an experimental image is not, and that distinction is where retractions happen.

Things this page does not answer

  • Your specific journal. Publisher rules are a starting point. Cell, for example, adds a specific cover-art restriction to Elsevier’s policy. Check the journal instructions as well.
  • Your thesis. University rules apply separately. Check the rules for your programme.
  • Your co-authors. Agree what the declaration says before submission — it describes the whole manuscript.
  • Your funder and preprint server. Their rules apply to different stages of the work. Their records are kept separate from publisher/journal agreement.
  • Cover letters and responses to reviewers. Check the named journal. Permission to edit a response does not authorize uploading a confidential review report.

What to actually do

Keep the record. That is the part you cannot recreate.

Start a log, even a rough one

Model and version, rough dates, the prompts, and the session records themselves. Write the declaration while the work is fresh — a first draft takes ten minutes to write. A transcript from eight months ago cannot be rebuilt, and model versions get retired.

Already required, not hypothetical: ISTIC Guideline, JAMA Network, JMIR Publications, Wiley require prompts, versions or archived session records today.

You already used it and kept no record

Write down what you can still remember — the tool, roughly which version, when, and which parts of the work it touched. Do it now: model versions change and chat histories disappear. Then follow the journal’s disclosure instructions. You do not owe anyone an apology in that sentence.

You already found a fabricated reference in your own draft

Catching a fabricated reference before submission is the system working. Remove or replace it, check every remaining reference against the original source, and carry on. If the error has already been published, contact the journal so the record can be put right.

Save a copy of the policy on the day you follow it

Keep the page, its URL and the date you checked it alongside your research notes. Check it again before submission. A dated copy helps you explain which guidance you followed if the wording changes in between.

Method

What this does and does not claim

Word for word, or not at all

Every verdict carries a short quote, its source, and how it was obtained. All 140 quotes on this page are checked automatically against the stored copy of the page they came from; the build fails if any quote cannot be found. An earlier version of this site carried a sentence in quotation marks that turned out to be a research assistant’s paraphrase. That is what this check exists to prevent.

Published policy only

This records what organisations have published. It cannot see what editors actually do. Across seventeen cardiovascular journals studied, fifteen ran AI-detection software and one said so publicly — the gap between stated and practised is real, and this site does not close it.

Coverage, stated plainly

36 organisations. Coverage is recorded activity by activity. If your journal, funder or institution has no entry, that means it has not been assessed — not that it is silent.

Where the evidence comes from

38 of 53 sources are fetched directly. 5 retain dated Internet Archive captures; their current pages are checked separately and carry a live-check date. 10 uses a saved excerpt from the rendered browser page, labelled as such. A fixed archive capture does not establish that the live policy is unchanged. Missing live checks enter the monitoring queue.

Contradictions, counted honestly

0 substantive internal contradictions and 4 dating or tagging oddities across 36 organisations. A stale HTML title tag is not a policy contradiction and is not counted as one. Differences in audience or product scope are kept separate.

0 cells still await a quote

Verdicts recorded without captured wording are marked and published, never asserted as verified. Where we could not find wording at all, the activity remains unassessed rather than being labelled silent.

This page declares itself unmaintained automatically once its oldest record passes 550 days without re-checking. The closest precedent to this project still serves a live page, with no staleness warning, four years after its last update.

Corrections and right of reply

If you work for an organisation recorded here and think a record is wrong, we want to fix it. Every record names its source and quotes the wording it relies on, so corrections can be specific. Open an issue and we will respond. Corrected records carry the date they changed, and the change stays in the public history.
