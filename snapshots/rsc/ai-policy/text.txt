Artificial Intelligence: authorship
We follow the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) Position Statement on Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Authorship. Artificial intelligence (AI) tools, such as ChatGPT or other Large Language Models, cannot be listed as an author on a submitted work. AI tools do not meet the criteria to qualify for authorship, as they are unable to take responsibility for the work, cannot consent to publication nor manage copyright, licence or other legal obligations, and are unable to understand issues around conflicts of interest.
Authors are fully responsible and accountable for the content of their article, including any parts produced by an AI tool.
Artificial Intelligence: disclosure and transparency
To ensure openness and clarity for our community, we have set out artificial intelligence policies guided by the principles shared by STM and the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) in their position statement.
We do not permit the presentation of any kind of content generated by AI tools as though it were original research data/results from nonmachine sources.
If GenAI tools have been used when generating any part of the manuscript (for example when drafting text, translating content, formatting data, creating or editing figures, visualising results, refining code, or compiling references) this must be declared at submission within the cover letter. Authors should also include a statement within the Experimental/Materials and Methods section outlining how the tool was used, including prompts. Details of the AI tool such as the name and specific model or version (e.g., GPT-4 or Claude 3.5 Sonnet) should be included in the Acknowledgements section. If there is no Experimental/Materials and Methods section, all details must be included in the Acknowledgements section. The use of an LLM (or other AI-tool) for “AI assisted copy editing” purposes does not need to be declared.
Recommended statement in Acknowledgements section:
“During the preparation of this manuscript/study, the author(s) used [tool name, version information] for the purposes of [description of use]. The authors have reviewed and edited the output and take full responsibility for the content of this publication.”
If AI usage is suspected and not disclosed, the RSC reserves the right to contact the author for further information. If concerns are not addressed satisfactorily, the manuscript may be withdrawn, rejected or retracted.
Artificial Intelligence: image creation and editing
Any AI tool used during image creation should be trained on licensed datasets and the output must be cleared for commercial reuse. Authors are fully responsible and accountable for the output and should check its accuracy.
Where graphics have been created using AI for illustrative or aesthetic purposes only, a brief description of AI use should be included in the figure caption to explain to readers how the image was created. Details of the AI tool such as the name and specific model or version should be included in the Acknowledgements section. Images containing identifiable people which have been generated using any AI tool are not acceptable. A replacement image without people or with non-identifiable people should be used.
GenAI or AI-assisted tools must not be used to alter images in submitted manuscripts. This includes adding, removing, moving, or obscuring features within an image or figure. Basic adjustments such as brightness, contrast or colour balance are acceptable, provided they do not hide or remove original information. We may use image forensics or specialist software to check for irregularities.
The only exception is where AI tools form part of the research design or methods, for example, AI-assisted imaging used to generate or interpret research data. In these cases, authors must describe the process clearly in the Experimental/Materials and Methods section, including how the tool was used, its name, version, and manufacturer. Authors should follow the tool’s usage policies and ensure correct attribution. Where requested, authors may need to provide original, unaltered images or raw composites for editorial review.
