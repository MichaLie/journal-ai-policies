November 6, 2026 ACS Publications recognizes the growing use of AI tools and supports their limited and responsible application in scientific publishing. Authors, editors, and peer reviewers must use AI transparently and ethically. For the purpose of this policy, generative AI means any software tool that substantially modifies or creates new content, such as text, images, figures, code, or data, in response to user prompts or inputs. Tools that only check spelling, grammar, or formatting without generating new content are not generative AI for purposes of this policy. Use of AI by Authors Authors may use generative AI tools to assist in preparing their submissions, provided that the use complies with this policy and is disclosed where required below. Permitted uses include improving the language, readability, or organization of the authors’ own text; translating text; and supporting literature searches. Authors must review and verify all AI-assisted content. Authors may not use generative AI to create, alter, or enhance (i) a paper’s underlying data or results; (ii) any image that represents research results; (iii) the substance of their scientific reasoning, analysis, or interpretation of results; or (iv) substantial amounts of commentary, analysis, or literature review content. Editors have discretion to determine whether the use of generative AI in a submission is inconsistent with this policy. The above restriction does not apply where AI or machine learning is part of the research methodology (e.g., predictive modeling, AI-based image reconstruction, or AI-predicted structures), provided the use is fully described in the Methods section and the original data are made available to editors and reviewers on request. Standard image processing is permitted if it is applied to the entire image, does not obscure, remove, or introduce features, and is disclosed in the Methods section or figure caption. Required Disclosures Authors must disclose the use of generative AI tools to create or substantially modify text, permitted images and figures, or other submission content. Disclosures must identify the AI tool used (including the version or model, where available), the purpose of its use, and the portion(s) of the manuscript affected. Brief disclosures should be included in the Acknowledgments section. More extensive use of generative AI must be described in the Methods section or another appropriate section of the manuscript. For AI-generated images or graphics, authors must also include a statement in the relevant figure caption describing how the content was created. Journal Cover Art AI-generated artwork is permitted for journal covers, provided authors must disclose their use of generative AI, comply with all other terms of this policy, and confirm that: The AI tool’s terms of use allow commercial use The authors hold enough rights to grant ACS rights to use the art on a journal cover The artwork does not infringe third-party rights (e.g., the artwork must not copy an artist’s work or contain a trademark, copyrighted work, or a real person’s likeness) Table of Contents (ToC) Graphics AI-generated images cannot be used in ToC graphics, as there is no accompanying caption or disclosure mechanism to provide transparent attribution and explain how AI was used. Author Responsibility Authors remain fully responsible for their submission, including any AI-generated material contained within it. Authors must ensure that all submitted content is accurate, non-infringing, and free from bias, and that it does not plagiarize others’ work and contains appropriate attribution or citation. AI tools do not qualify for authorship, as they cannot assume responsibility or accountability for published work. As such, AI tools must not be listed as authors. An author’s failure to comply with or violation of this policy may be considered a breach of the ACS Ethical Guidelines to Publication of Chemical Research and may result in editorial action. Such actions may include manuscript rejection, withdrawal, correction, expression of concern, retraction, or notification of institutions or funding agencies, as appropriate to the nature and severity of the concern. Use of AI by Editors and Peer Reviewers ACS Publications relies on the expert assessments of its editors and invited peer reviewers. Editors remain responsible for all editorial assessments and decisions and must not delegate editorial judgment or decision-making to a generative AI tool. Similarly, peer reviewers remain responsible for all comments and recommendations provided during peer review and must not use generative AI tools to generate, draft, or revise their peer review reports. Submissions, associated data, peer review reports, author and reviewer correspondence, and other materials obtained through the editorial and peer review processes constitute confidential materials. Editors and peer reviewers must maintain the confidentiality of such materials and must not upload, share, or disclose them to AI tools or platforms other than those that ACS has authorized and integrated into its editorial or peer review system(s) (“ACS-Authorized Tools”). An editor’s or peer reviewer’s use of AI tools in a manner that does not comply with this policy also constitutes a violation of the ACS Ethical Guidelines to Publication of Chemical Research . Where confidential materials are uploaded, shared, or disclosed to an AI tool or platform other than an ACS-Authorized Tool, such use will constitute a breach of confidentiality and violation of this policy.
