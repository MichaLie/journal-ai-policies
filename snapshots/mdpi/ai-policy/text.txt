MDPI recognizes the opportunities and challenges presented by the rapid development of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) such as ChatGPT and other large language models (LLMs) for scholarly publishing.
MDPI’s policy on the use of AI tools is informed by the STM and the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) position statement.
The underlying principles of this policy are the need for transparency, reproducibility and accountability.
Artificial intelligence (AI) tools cannot be listed as an author because they cannot be held responsible for the accuracy, integrity and originality of the work. Human authors are responsible for the entire content of their manuscript, including the output of any AI tools that might have been used.
Transparency and reproducibility
Authors are expected to declare all use of AI tools* (generative and others) during the conduct and reporting of the research in the manuscript.
Any AI tools that were used must be declared in the cover letter on submission of the manuscript.
To support reproducibility and enable other researchers to repeat the study, authors must provide details of how the tools were used in the conduct of their study in the ‘Methods’ section of their manuscript. For example, if an AI tool was used to analyze or summarize data, authors should specify the tool used, what prompts were used, the outputs generated by the tools and the ways in which the outputs were edited or modified. Authors are encouraged to submit the raw outputs of the AI tools they used as supplementary files.
Details of all the tools used, including those used to help write the manuscript should be included in a statement in the ‘Acknowledgments’ section. The prompts used should be described and the product version of the tool should be included. An example of such a statement is shown below.
“During the preparation of this manuscript/study, the author(s) used [tool name, version information] for the purposes of [description of use]. The prompts used were [include the prompts that were given to the AI tool]. The authors have reviewed and edited the output and take full responsibility for the content of this publication.”
*The use of GenAI tools for the purposes of text editing (e.g., grammar, structure, spelling, punctuation and formatting) is not covered by this policy and does not need to be declared.
Accountability
GenAI tools cannot be listed as an author of a manuscript because AI cannot be held responsible for the accuracy and integrity of the work, as per our requirements for [Authorship].
Authors are fully responsible for the accuracy, validity, and integrity of the content of their manuscript, regardless of any material contributed by generative or other AI tools. It is the authors’ responsibility to ensure that any AI-generated material (both during the conduct and reporting of their research) complies with all of MDPI’s core and journal-specific editorial policies.
Unacceptable uses
GenAI tools must not be used:
To generate, alter, or manipulate research data, results, or images in a way that could misrepresent the underlying research.
To generate images purporting to represent data that does not exist or that was also generated by an AI tool, or to alter or manipulate original images.
If GenAI tools are used to generate figures to represent original raw data, the raw data and details of the tools and prompts used should be described in the manuscript.
If GenAI tools are used to generate images to represent an abstract (e.g., graphic abstract), details of the tools and prompts used should be described within the manuscript.
The inclusion of AI-generated images for purposes other than data visualization is at the discretion of the journal’s Editorial Board.
In all situations, authors should make sure they understand the terms and conditions of the GenAI tools they use. Authors are responsible for ensuring that uploading any material to the tool does not breach any confidentiality, privacy, or copyright restrictions. Authors must also ensure that they do not, inadvertently or intentionally, agree to the re-use of the material they uploaded to train the tool, unless due safeguards to protect copyright and rights to confidentiality and privacy are in place.
Review articles
While the use of GenAI tools may be acceptable to assist with the preparation of manuscripts under some circumstances, MDPI is aware that some authors use GenAI tools to fabricate entire manuscripts. This is especially true for the ‘narrative review’ article type and can also occur for systematic reviews. For both narrative and systematic reviews, authors are expected to define the question that the review is attempting to address, the search strategy, and the criteria used for selecting the included articles. Further requirements for systematic review articles are provided under MDPI’s ‘Guidelines for Conducting Systematic Reviews’.
Regardless of the article type, authors are strongly encouraged to maintain a record of the provenance of their manuscripts, even for secondary research, non-quantitative research, and opinion-based articles so that the authenticity of their work can be proven, should it be called into question.
Time-stamped previous versions of the manuscript should be kept, preferably in a third-party repository, such as the Open Science Framework. These do not need to be made openly available to the public, but authors should be prepared to share previous versions with the journal editor or Publication Ethics and Research Integrity Team upon request.
GenAI use by reviewers
GenAI tools and other large language models (LLMs) should not be used by reviewers in the preparation of review reports. Reviewers are solely responsible for the contents of their reports, and the utilization of these tools may violate confidentiality, proprietary, and data privacy rights. Some limited use to improve the written quality of a peer review report, such as checking grammar, structure, spelling, punctuation, and formatting, may be acceptable but should be disclosed upon submission of the peer review report. Under no circumstances should reviewers upload manuscripts either in whole or in part, images, figures, tables, or any kind of communication relating to manuscripts that are under review to any GenAI tools, as this violates MDPI's confidentiality policy relating to peer review. If it is determined that AI tools have been inappropriately used in the preparation of a review report, the report will be discarded.
GenAI use in editorial decision-making
Academic Editors (Editors-in-Chief, Editorial Board Members, and Guest Editors) play an essential role within the peer review process to ensure the integrity and rigor of this process. Given this significant responsibility, Academic Editors must not use GenAI tools during the editorial review or decision-making process. Under no circumstances should Academic Editors upload manuscripts either in whole or in part, images, figures, tables, or any kind of communication relating to manuscripts that are under review to any GenAI tools, as this violates MDPI's confidentiality policy relating to peer review.
Note: These guidelines are intended to reflect current best practices regarding the use of artificial intelligence (AI) and large language models (LLMs) in research and scholarly publishing. Given the rapid evolution of these technologies, this guidance will be reviewed and may be updated periodically. Authors are responsible for ensuring compliance with the most up-to-date version of this policy.
