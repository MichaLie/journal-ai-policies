AI disclosure in research: towards a global reporting standard Theme: Artificial Intelligence , Digitalization Status: In progress Scroll down While Artificial Intelligence (AI) can play a valuable role in research, its use must remain transparent, traceable, and responsibly integrated within scholarly practices. Help us develop a global reporting standard for AI disclosure in research. Background Transparency about the use of AI in research articles and other scholarly outputs is an important aspect of research integrity. At present, policies and practices for how to disclose AI use vary widely across disciplines, regions and publication cultures. Many publishers have their own disclosure requirements. These developments underscore the need for a shared, global understanding of how AI contributions to research should be made transparent. To address this need, the International Science Council (ISC), the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) , the Association of Scientific, Technical and Medical Publishers (STM) and the Global Young Academy (GYA) have taken the joint initiative to work toward a Global Reporting Standard for AI Disclosure in Research . This work aims to develop a broadly supported reporting standard that can be used across research disciplines, publication cultures and organisational contexts. Similar to established reporting tools, such as the Contributor Role Taxonomy (CRediT) , such a standard would help align expectations across the research ecosystem, make disclosure practices more consistent and comparable, and facilitate implementation by publishers, institutions and researchers. By harmonizing AI disclosure, we hope to support transparency, reduce uncertainty among authors, and ultimately strengthen research integrity. This initiative formed the focus track of the World Conference on Research Integrity in Vancouver, held in May 2026. In two 90 minutes sessions, participants discussed thresholds for AI disclosure in a position game ( slides of session 2.B on thresholds ) and documenting AI oversight and verification processes ( slides of session 4.B on accountability ). Activities and impact To ensure that the development of this reporting standard reflects a wide range of perspectives, the ISC, WCRIF, COPE, STM and GYA invite their members and the broader research community to contribute through three consultation rounds : December 2025 – February 2026 : Mapping the needs for AI disclosure, yielding a preferred structured format ( questions and preparatory reading ). July 2026 – October 2026: Identifying when, where and how AI use should be disclosed ( questions and preparatory reading ) Early 2027 : Refining the Vancouver Standard based on concrete feedback. This participatory approach brings together perspectives from editors, academy members, research institutions leadership, funders, libraries, ethics bodies, publishers and experts in research integrity. How to participate The second consultation round is now closed. We thank those publishers, institutions, funders and other organisations, as well as the individuals who contributed to consultation rounds 1 and 2. We plan to open the third consultation round in January 2027, on this webpage. This initiative was proposed to the World Conference on Research Integrity 2026 by Kari D. Weaver (Program Manager, AI and Machine Learning and Learning, Teaching, and Instructional Design Librarian, Ontario Council of University Libraries ) and Bert Seghers (President of ENRIO, European Network of Research Integrity Offices ). ISC, COPE, STM and the GYA are ambassadors of this track. It is supported by ENRIO and ISC staff. To get in touch, contact Bert Seghers at [email protected] or Felix Dijkstal at [email protected] .
